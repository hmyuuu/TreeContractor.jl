## Theoretical Analysis (Deep Dive)
- **Oum-Seymour Algorithm:** Identified that the "Approximation" relies on minimizing the cut-rank function. Since cut-rank is **symmetric submodular**, we can use **Queyranne's Algorithm** (1998) to find the minimum rank cut in $O(n^3)$ time without needing complex matroid reductions. This provides a concrete path for the "Exact Split" implementation.
- **Hypergraph Partitioning (Gray 2018):** Confirmed that modern TN solvers (Cotengra/KaHyPar) minimize "Carving Width" (edge cuts). Rank-Width is a "Semantic Carving Width" that accounts for linear dependencies. This validates our hypothesis that Rank-Width is the superior metric for structured tensors.
- **Fast FPT Algorithm (Korhonen 2024):** Analyzed the "Dynamic Rank-Width" algorithm ($O(n^{1+o(1)})$). Concluded it is theoretically ground-breaking but practically overkill for $N < 500$ compared to $O(n^3)$ Queyranne. We will stick to the $O(n^3)$ approach for implementation.

## Benchmark Results (Hypothesis H1)
**Experiment:** Compared `LinearRankWidth` (BFS ordering) vs `BranchingRankWidth` (Random Balanced Splits) on Random Graphs and Perfect Binary Trees.

**Findings:**
1.  **Random Graphs:** Both methods yield high rank-width (~N/2), consistent with theory. Branching shows a negligible advantage (3-10%).
2.  **Trees:**
    -   Theory predicts `RW(Tree) = 1` and `LRW(Tree) = O(log n)`.
    -   **Simulation:**
        -   Linear Heuristic (BFS) yields `~N/4` (e.g., 32 for N=127). Poor performance.
        -   Branching Heuristic (Random) yields `~N/4` (e.g., 33 for N=127). **Failed to exploit tree structure.**
3.  **Conclusion:** Random balanced splits are insufficient for finding low rank-width decompositions in structured graphs like trees. This strongly motivates the need for the **Oum-Seymour** approximation algorithm (or connectivity-based splitting) rather than naive heuristics. The gap between "Theory" and "Naive Heuristic" is massive.
