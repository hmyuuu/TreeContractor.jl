## 1. Group Meeting Summary
**Date:** 2025-12-22
**Participants:** Principal Investigator (PI), Quantum Physicist (QP), Algorithm Engineer (AE)

**Discussion Log:**
*   **QP:** This paper introduces "Tesseract", a decoder for Quantum LDPC codes (like bivariate bicycle codes). It uses A* search.
*   **AE:** LDPC codes are expanders, so they have high Rank-Width ($O(n)$). Our DP solver ($2^{k^2}$) would die on them.
*   **QP:** True, but Tesseract works "in practice" for random errors. It's a heuristic.
*   **PI:** Where do we fit in?
*   **AE:** Two places:
    1.  **Benchmarking**: For small codes or low-error regimes, the effective error graph might be disconnected or low-width. We can use our solver to provide the *Ground Truth* (Exact MLD) to benchmark Tesseract's accuracy.
    2.  **Surface Codes**: Surface codes have $k \approx \sqrt{n}$. For moderate sizes ($d=5, 7$), our solver is efficient. Tesseract compares itself to BP+OSD. We should be the "Exact Baseline".
*   **QP:** Also, "Linear Rank-Width" is equivalent to the path-width of the Tanner graph. If we optimize the ordering of checks, we might improve Tesseract's heuristic function.

**Decisions:**
1.  **Position Rank-Width Solver as an "Exact MLD Verifier"** for small/medium quantum codes.
2.  **Investigate "Syndrome Graph"**: If the error model creates a graph, computing its Rank-Width can predict if decoding will be easy.

## 2. Deep Analysis

### 2.1 The Problem: MLD
Maximum Likelihood Decoding asks: Given a syndrome $s$, find the error $E$ with minimum weight such that $H \cdot E = s$.
*   This is equivalent to **Minimum Weight Perfect Matching** (for surface codes) or generic **Min-Weight Solution to Linear Equations**.
*   In our terminology: It's a `Min-Cut` or `Shortest Path` problem on a graph.

### 2.2 Tesseract's Approach
*   **Method**: A* Search on the "Error Graph".
*   **Heuristic**: Likely uses Belief Propagation (BP) or local structure to estimate cost.
*   **Limitation**: Runtime is non-deterministic and can explode for complex error patterns (high rank-width equivalent).

### 2.3 Rank-Width Connection
*   Our solver (T-027, T-032) performs exact contraction.
*   For a code with rank-width $k$, we can solve MLD exactly in $2^{O(k)}$.
*   **Bivariate Bicycle Codes**: These are "thick" (high width). We cannot solve them generally.
*   **Surface/Color Codes**: These are 2D manifolds. $k \approx \sqrt{n}$. We *can* solve them for $n \approx 100$ ($k \approx 10$).

## 3. Unresearched Opportunities
*   **Hybrid Decoder**: Use Tesseract (A*) for the "bulk" of the high-width graph, but use **Rank-Width DP** to resolve dense local clusters (small "islands" of high errors).
*   **Pre-computation**: Use `LinearRankWidth.jl` to find an optimal *elimination order* for the check variables. This is a static optimization that could speed up the dynamic A* search.
