# Analysis: Rank-Width and Vertex-Minors (Oum 2005)

**Paper:** "Rank-Width and Vertex-Minors"
**Author:** Sang-il Oum
**Year:** 2005 (Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B, Vol. 95, pp. 79-100)
**Task:** T-068
**Agent:** PhD-Theory

## 1. Historical Significance

This is THE foundational paper establishing vertex-minors as the "right" notion of graph containment for rank-width, analogous to how graph minors characterize tree-width. Key contributions:

1. **Vertex-minor relation** for rank-width (analogous to graph minors for tree-width)
2. **Excluded vertex-minor characterization** with bounded obstruction size
3. **Connection to binary matroids** via fundamental graphs
4. **Distance-hereditary graphs** = rank-width at most 1

## 2. The Motivation: Finding "Minor" for Clique-Width

**Problem:** Clique-width has nice algorithmic properties, but no good minor relation exists.

**Why graph minors don't work:**
- Every graph $G$ is a minor of $K_{|V(G)|}$
- But $K_n$ has clique-width 2 for $n > 1$
- So minor containment says nothing about clique-width!

**Solution:** Use **vertex-minors** which respect rank-width.

## 3. Core Definitions

### 3.1 Cut-Rank
For graph $G$ with adjacency matrix $M$ over GF(2):
$$\text{cutrk}_G(A) = \text{rank}(M[A, V(G) \setminus A])$$

### 3.2 Local Complementation
For $v \in V(G)$, the **local complementation at $v$** produces:
$$G * v = (V, E \triangle \{xy : xv, yv \in E, x \neq y\})$$

**Effect:** Replace the induced subgraph on $N(v)$ with its complement.

### 3.3 Pivoting
For edge $uv \in E(G)$:
$$G \wedge uv = G * u * v * u = G * v * u * v$$

**Proposition 2.1 (Pivoting Directly):**
Let $V_1 = N(u) \cap N(v)$, $V_2 = N(u) \setminus N(v) \setminus \{v\}$, $V_3 = N(v) \setminus N(u) \setminus \{u\}$.

Then $G \wedge uv$ is obtained by:
1. Toggle edges between $(V_1, V_2)$, $(V_2, V_3)$, $(V_3, V_1)$
2. Exchange vertices $u$ and $v$ (swap their neighborhoods)

### 3.4 Vertex-Minor
$H$ is a **vertex-minor** of $G$ if $H$ can be obtained by:
- Sequence of local complementations
- Vertex deletions

**Corollary 2.7 (Monotonicity):** If $H$ is a vertex-minor of $G$, then $\text{rwd}(H) \leq \text{rwd}(G)$.

## 4. Connection to Binary Matroids

### 4.1 Fundamental Graph
For binary matroid $M$ with basis $B$:
- $\text{Bin}(G, A, B)$ = binary matroid on $V(G)$ represented by $(I_A | M[A, B])$
- $G$ is a **fundamental graph** of $M$

### 4.2 Key Correspondence

**Proposition 3.1:**
$$\lambda_M(X) = \text{cutrk}_G(X) + 1$$

**Corollary 3.2:**
$$\text{branch-width}(M) = \text{rank-width}(G) + 1$$

### 4.3 Minors and Vertex-Minors

**Proposition 3.3:**
1. $\text{Bin}(G, B, A) = M^*$ (dual)
2. $\text{Bin}(G \wedge uv, A \triangle \{u,v\}, B \triangle \{u,v\}) = M$ (pivoting preserves matroid)
3. Deletion/contraction in $M$ ↔ pivoting + deletion in $G$

**Corollary 3.6:**
- Minors of binary matroids → pivot-minors of fundamental graphs
- Pivot-minors preserve the matroid structure

## 5. Excluded Vertex-Minors Theorem

### 5.1 Main Result

**Theorem 5.4:**
> If $G$ has rank-width $> k$ but every proper pivot-minor has rank-width $\leq k$, then:
> $$|V(G)| \leq \frac{6^{k+1} - 1}{5}$$

**Corollary 5.5 (Excluded Vertex-Minor Characterization):**
For each $k \geq 0$, there exists a **finite** list $\mathcal{C}_k$ of graphs with at most:
$$c_k = \begin{cases} (6^{k+1} - 1)/5 & \text{if } k > 0 \\ 2 & \text{if } k = 0 \end{cases}$$

such that $\text{rwd}(G) \leq k$ iff no graph in $\mathcal{C}_k$ is isomorphic to a vertex-minor of $G$.

### 5.2 Proof Technique

Uses **$k$-branched** sets and $(m, g)$-connectivity:
- Submodular inequalities on cut-rank
- Induction on $(m, g)$-connectivity levels
- Key lemma: Either $G \setminus v$ or $G \wedge vw \setminus v$ is $(m, 2g)$-connected

## 6. Tutte's Linking Theorem Analog

**Theorem 6.1:**
For graph $G$ and disjoint $X, Y \subseteq V(G)$, the following are equivalent:
1. $\min_{X \subseteq Z \subseteq V(G) \setminus Y} \text{cutrk}_G(Z) \leq k$
2. $\exists$ vertex-minor $G'$ with $V(G') = X \cup Y$ and $\text{cutrk}_{G'}(X) \leq k$
3. $\exists$ pivot-minor $G'$ with $V(G') = X \cup Y$ and $\text{cutrk}_{G'}(X) \leq k$

**Implication:** Finding minimum cut-rank separation is in NP ∩ coNP.

## 7. Distance-Hereditary Graphs

### 7.1 Definition
$G$ is **distance-hereditary** iff for every connected induced subgraph $H$, distances in $H$ equal distances in $G$.

### 7.2 Characterization

**Proposition 7.3:**
$$G \text{ is distance-hereditary} \iff \text{rwd}(G) \leq 1$$

**Proof uses:**
- **Twins:** $v, w$ are twins if $N(v) \setminus \{w\} = N(w) \setminus \{v\}$
- **Proposition 7.1:** Deleting a twin doesn't change rank-width
- **Proposition 7.2:** Rank-width ≤ 1 graphs have twins or pendant vertices
- **Bandelt-Mulder characterization** of distance-hereditary graphs

**Corollary 7.4:** Distance-hereditary graphs have clique-width at most 3.

### 7.3 Excluded Vertex-Minor for Rank-Width 1

**Bouchet's Theorem:** $G$ is distance-hereditary iff $C_5$ is NOT a vertex-minor of $G$.

So $\mathcal{C}_1 = \{C_5\}$ (the 5-cycle is the unique obstruction).

## 8. Key Submodular Inequalities

### 8.1 Cut-Rank Submodularity
**Corollary 4.2:**
$$\text{cutrk}_G(X) + \text{cutrk}_G(Y) \geq \text{cutrk}_G(X \cap Y) + \text{cutrk}_G(X \cup Y)$$

### 8.2 Local Complementation Preserves Cut-Rank

**Proposition 2.6:**
$$\text{cutrk}_{G*v}(X) = \text{cutrk}_G(X) \quad \forall X \subseteq V(G)$$

**Proof:** Local complementation = row operations on adjacency matrix.

### 8.3 Key Inequality for Proofs

**Lemma 4.4:**
$$\text{cutrk}_{G \setminus v}(X_1) + \text{cutrk}_{G \wedge vw \setminus v}(Y_1) \leq \text{cutrk}_G(X_1 \cap Y_1) + \text{cutrk}_G(X_2 \cap Y_2) - 1$$

## 9. Connection to Quantum Information

### 9.1 Graph States
A **graph state** $|G\rangle$ is a stabilizer state defined by the adjacency matrix.

### 9.2 Local Clifford Equivalence
Two graph states are **LC-equivalent** iff their graphs are related by local complementations.

**Key Insight:**
- **Rank-width** measures entanglement complexity **invariant under local Clifford operations**
- This is "information-theoretic" complexity vs. tree-width's "topological" complexity

### 9.3 Pivoting = Basis Change
Pivoting $G \wedge uv$ corresponds to changing the computational basis of qubits $u$ and $v$.

## 10. Algorithmic Implications

### 10.1 Polynomial-Time Recognition
Combined with Courcelle & Oum (2007):
- Vertex-minor containment is expressible in MS logic
- For fixed $k$, deciding rank-width $\leq k$ is polynomial-time

### 10.2 Grid Theorem for Bipartite Graphs

**Corollary 3.9 (from Geelen-Gerards-Whittle):**
For every $k$, there exists $\ell$ such that bipartite graphs with rank-width $\geq \ell$ contain a specific structured graph $S_k$ as a vertex-minor.

**Open Problem:** Does this hold for general (non-bipartite) graphs?

## 11. Well-Quasi-Ordering

**Oum (2004, referenced):** Graphs of bounded rank-width are **well-quasi-ordered** by vertex-minor relation.

This generalizes Geelen-Gerards-Whittle's theorem that binary matroids of bounded branch-width are WQO by minor relation.
