## 1. The Core Problem

Standard approaches to DP on rank-decompositions convert them to **Clique-Width Expressions** (k-expressions).
- **Critical Flaw:** Rank-width $k$ → Clique-width up to $2^{k+1} - 1$
- **Result:** Exponential blowup in the parameter makes algorithms impractical

**This paper's contribution:** Direct DP on rank-decompositions without conversion.

## 2. Labeling Parse Trees (Definition 2.5)

### The Core Formalism
A **t-labeling parse tree** $T$ consists of:

1. **Nullary operator** $\bigcirc$: Creates a single vertex with label $\{1\}$
2. **Binary composition** $\otimes[g | f_1, f_2]$:
   - Takes two t-labeled graphs $\bar{G}_1, \bar{G}_2$
   - Applies t-relabeling $g$ to $\bar{G}_2$
   - Adds edges: $\{u, v\}$ iff $lab_1(u) \cdot g(lab_2(v)) = 1$ over GF(2)
   - New labels: $f_1 \circ lab_1$ and $f_2 \circ lab_2$

### Key Insight
The **join** operator is exactly the bilinear product over GF(2):
$$\{u,v\} \in E \iff lab_1(u) \times T_g^T \times lab_2(v)^T = 1$$

## 3. The Rank-Width Parsing Theorem (Theorem 2.6)

**Theorem:** A graph $G$ has rank-width at most $t$ iff some labeling of $G$ can be generated by a t-labeling parse tree.

**Algorithmic Implication:** A width-$t$ rank-decomposition can be transformed into a t-labeling parse tree in time $O(t^2 \cdot |V(G)|^2)$.

**This is exactly what our `ParseTrees.jl` implements!**

## 4. Canonical Equivalence (Definition 3.3)

For a graph class $D$ and $D_t = D \cap R_t$ (graphs in $D$ with rank-width ≤ t):

$$\bar{G}_1 \approx_{D,t} \bar{G}_2 \iff \forall \bar{H} \in R_t: (\bar{G}_1 \otimes \bar{H} \in D_t \Leftrightarrow \bar{G}_2 \otimes \bar{H} \in D_t)$$

**Interpretation:** Two labeled subgraphs are equivalent if they "behave the same" when joined with any other graph.

## 5. Rank-Width Regularity Theorem (Theorem 3.4)

**THE FUNDAMENTAL RESULT:**

> The collection of all t-labeling parse trees generating members of $D_t$ is accepted by a **finite tree automaton** iff the canonical equivalence $\approx_{D,t}$ has **finite index**.

**Consequence:** There's a natural bijection between automaton states and canonical equivalence classes.

## 6. MSO₁ Properties (Theorem 4.2)

**Extended Canonical Equivalence:** For any MS₁ formula $\phi$ with free variables, the equivalence $\approx_{\phi,t}^\sigma$ has **finite index**.

**Corollary 4.8:** If $F$ is a graph class definable in MS₁, then the language of parse trees generating rank-width-$t$ members of $F$ is accepted by a finite tree automaton.

## 7. LinEMSO Optimization (Theorem 4.13)

**Problem:** Given MS₁ formula $\psi(X_1, \ldots, X_p)$ and linear objective:
$$f_{lin}(U_1, \ldots, U_p) = \sum_{i,j} a_{i,j} \sum_{x \in U_i} f_j(x)$$

**Result:** Solvable in **linear time** $O(|V(G)|)$ for fixed $t$.

**Examples:** Maximum Independent Set, Minimum Dominating Set, Minimum Vertex Cover.

## 8. Prepartitioned Canonical Equivalence (PCE) Scheme

**Definition 5.2:** A refined framework that separates:
- $B_t$: Partition capturing "expectations" of future data
- $A_t^B$: Partition of remembered information relative to expectation $B$

**Key Insight:** Simultaneous tracking of:
1. Current fragment of solution
2. "Expectation" of the rest

This allows **single-exponential** algorithms where ordinary canonical equivalence would give towers of exponentials.

## 9. Concrete Algorithm Runtimes

| Problem | Runtime | Notes |
|---------|---------|-------|
| Independent Set | $O(2^{t(t+1)/2} \cdot t^3 \cdot n)$ | Uses subspace counting |

**Key Bound (Proposition 6.1):** Number of subspaces of $GF(2)^t$ is at most $2^{t(t+1)/4} - 2$ for $t \geq 12$.

## 11. Distance-Hereditary Graphs

**Fact (from paper):** Distance-hereditary graphs have **rank-width 1**.

This explains why distance-hereditary graphs have "nice" algorithmic properties - they're the simplest non-trivial rank-width class.

**Connection to Bouchet 1987:** Distance-hereditary = totally decomposable in isotropic system theory.
